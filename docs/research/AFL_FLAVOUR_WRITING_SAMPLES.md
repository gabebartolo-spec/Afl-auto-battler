# Original flavour samples and content rules

Companion to [the research report](AFL_FLAVOUR_AND_CULTURE_RESEARCH.md). These are original prototype examples, not implemented copy, real-player quotations or approved execution tasks. All quantities and pacing are prototype assumptions.

## Voice

Warm, specific, economical. The football should carry the drama. Use dry humour occasionally; let meaningful achievements remain sincere. Instructions stay plain.

Example distinction:

- Action: **Return to club**
- Optional atmosphere, only beside a supported home return: **Back at our place.**
- Avoid: an obscure slang button whose purpose must be guessed.

Names in templates mean the current displayed real/fictive name. Do not leak real identity through a nickname, image, imported anecdote or surname in fictive mode.

## 1. Match-day and banner copy

| Surface | Original example | Required truth / fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Existing pre-match transition | **Warming up** | Already supported phase. Do not claim loading complete |
| Existing pre-match transition | **Final instructions** | Existing huddle phase; no implied tactical bonus |
| Existing run-out | **Through the banner** | Current run-out. No additional waiting |
| Milestone banner | **200 games. Still one of us.** | Exactly 200 senior career games for a selected player; name accompanies it. If milestone conventions count the upcoming match, apply the existing milestone convention once |
| Club-tenure banner | **100 games in our colours.** | Club games, not league total; accurate display name and club |
| First senior appearance | **Welcome to senior footy, {display_name}.** | Genuine senior debut known from complete history, not merely first match in this save |
| Existing finals fixture | **Finals footy. Here we go.** | Fixture is actually a final; never put it on a late-season home-and-away match |
| Ordinary fixture fallback | **{club_name}** | Preserve the existing reliable fallback |

A caption should not promise dominance, a reward or a performance increase. A late omission cancels the player's banner. Long names wrap or use the ordinary club fallback; do not truncate someone into an unrecognisable nickname.

## 2. Short factual headlines

| Original headline | Required predicate | Plain alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **We made that interesting.** | Won narrowly after holding a clearly larger lead; thresholds explicitly defined and supported by saved score history | **Won by {margin} points** |
| **The neighbours heard that one.** | A real rivalry victory, used as figurative editorial copy, not measured sound/attendance | **Won against {opponent}** |
| **A good week to know a defender.** | Report is about a selected defender's verified notable defensive event/output; no invented unseen blocks | **{display_name}: {supported_fact}** |
| **First goal. Properly on the board.** | A genuine first senior goal, complete history available | **First senior goal for {display_name}** |
| **Not much room to breathe.** | Close final margin; says nothing about fatigue | **Decided by {margin} points** |
| **A long season, a place in the finals.** | The club actually qualifies, not merely remains mathematically possible | **Finals qualification confirmed** |
| **A year worth remembering.** | Only paired with specific true campaign beats; not automatically every season | **Your {season_year} season** |
| **The end of the road this year.** | Actual elimination from finals or completed season | **Season complete** |

These are authored editorial lines, never statements attributed to a real coach or player. Neutral loss reporting is usually preferable to a joke. A draw has its own neutral alternative. “Comeback”, “club record” and “career best” require stronger data than a favourable final score.

Keep the exact score and outcome easy to find. Flavour does not replace useful reporting, imply a best move, or explain causality without evidence.

## 3. Fictional profile details

Possible examples for explicitly fictional generated players:

- **Nickname: Moz** — user-editable cosmetic field, with the full name still primary.
- **Outside footy: restores old radios.**
- **Outside footy: grows tomatoes on a balcony.**
- **Outside footy: learns guitar, slowly.**
- **Outside footy: weekend birdwatcher.**
- **Outside footy: cooks for the extended family.**
- **Outside footy: sketchbook usually nearby.**

These interests are examples of variety, not statements about which activities are uniquely Australian. They must not correlate with potential, injury risk, work ethic, loyalty, teamwork or any other ability. A surfing interest grants no fitness; a cooking interest grants no morale.

Do not mechanically assign interests by ethnicity or hometown. Do not manufacture a family or pet whose later death becomes a surprise story hook. Leave some profiles without an extra detail. Selection and persistence use cosmetic identity independent of the football seed stream.

## 4. Actual history, expressed simply

| Original example | Required fact |
|---|---|
| **{display_name} · {club_games} games in our colours** | Actual club tenure count |
| **First wore our colours in {year}.** | Known club debut/stint year |
| **A premiership player, now on the coaching staff.** | Both facts actually true; current staff appointment, no automatic post-retirement hiring |
| **{year}: the year we broke the drought.** | Verified premiership and prior drought length/history |
| **{display_name}'s final season with the club.** | Known completed departure/retirement; do not announce an unconfirmed future |
| **Same club. Another generation.** | Decorative section title next to actual decades of history, without inventing kinship |

Imported “known history” and simulated alternative history should remain distinguishable where relevant. Do not call someone a lifelong one-club player after a transfer. Do not retain “youngster” indefinitely or fabricate a father-son connection because two players share a surname.

## 5. Environmental details without captions

Potential details in already-existing scenes:

- A banner's taped seam catches the light.
- Club-colour scarves sit among varied clothing, not a wall of identical supporters.
- A clubroom frame changes to the actual premiership year after a real win.
- A volunteer straightens a crooked, fictional general notice.
- The trophy presentation gives the winner a brief readable pause.
- A crowd reaction rises and then falls, leaving a little room after it.

These are optional art directions. They create no chores, currency, morale, attendance figures or extra decision prompts. Actual historic trophy shapes, club assets and cultural designs require their own sourced/usable assets; neutral original props suffice for prototypes.

Possible original notices on a fictional clubroom wall:

- **Please return the good scissors.**
- **Lost property: one boot. Owner still hopeful.**
- **Banner night: bring tape. More tape than that.**
- **Tea roster: volunteers welcome. Expert opinions already supplied.**

These are background prop jokes about ordinary club activity. They are not quests, player equipment losses, staff assignments or resource requirements. Use at most one readable notice in a prototype scene; the real controls remain visually primary.

## 6. Content selection contract

A future entry should record:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Stable content ID | Prevent duplicate displays and make revisions traceable |
| Surface and phase | Put it in an existing screen/scene at the right moment |
| Available-fact predicate | Explain exactly why the line is eligible |
| Displayed person/club identifiers | Avoid wrong names after transfers or fictive-mode changes |
| Plain fallback | Missing data must not generate invented history |
| Tone and exclusion tags | Keep humour away from injury, distress and significant ceremony |
| Repeat rule | Permit silence; don't cycle through the same joke every match |
| Asset/source provenance | Credit actual commissioned/licensed work where needed |

Keep this as a small authored presentation corpus. It is not approval for a dialogue generator, live external AI service, content economy or another news system.

## 7. Review standard

Read every line at phone size in context, with the real score and controls present. Ask whether it sounds natural, whether a newcomer understands it, whether it implies hidden effects, and whether it still feels welcome on the tenth encounter. Revise or remove lines that need an explanatory paragraph to make the joke work.

No samples require mandatory profanity, copied film catchphrases, song lyrics, brand slogans or fake real-player anecdotes. Genuine match drama and the player's actual history remain the source of emotional weight.
