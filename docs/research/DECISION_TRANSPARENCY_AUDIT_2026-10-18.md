# Decision transparency audit, 2026-10-18

Four read-only auditors (matchcalls, clubmgmt, listbuild, story) audited every decision gate in the game against the
director's change of philosophy. Nothing was run in Godot; every claim cites `file:line` on origin/main as the auditors read
it. Path shorthand: unless a path is given in full, `sim/` and `ui/` are under `scripts/`; GS = `scripts/state/GameState.gd`,
SE = `scripts/sim/StoryEngine.gd`, SC = `scripts/state/ClubStories.gd`, CL = `scripts/sim/ClubLife.gd`, CO =
`scripts/sim/Conditioning.gd`, PS = `scripts/sim/PreSeason.gd`, HS = `scripts/ui/HubScene.gd`. The four files' tables are in
the appendices (section 10) with every `file:line` kept. Nothing here changes the bible or any code; sections 7 and 8
are questions for the director.

## 1. His words, and what they mean

2026-10-18, 19:5x: "I've had a change in heart regarding my philosophy of only using football language to describe the
effects of levers. I still want the football language, but I also want full transparency behind each decision and the
impact it will have. How can I expect the players to make sound choices and have agency if theyre constantly rolling the
dice. To be clear, RNG is still okay, as long as the risks are clearly defined in the decision gate. Please audit the game
regarding this change."

2026-10-18, later the same evening: "I want to reverse this philosophy, but the decision should still be relatively opaque
and both options carrying positives and negatives."

What this means: football words stay. Every decision gate states, for each option, its upside and its downside, with the
size and the odds. The game never says which option is better and never reduces a choice to one number. An option with no
downside is a design fault, because there is nothing to weigh. Randomness is fine once the gate has named the risk.

## 6. Ship-first order (the lead's)

1. Key match-up cards show the ratings the contest runs on (G6, G16). Why: the largest single measured effect, about +3.45 points conceded on a bad match-up (docs/research/LEVER_TRUTH_AUDIT.md:127-139); the ratings are already in the rule, one line per card.
2. The in-match risk cards' multipliers in the copy: Stack, Throw numbers, Slow it down (G13, G14). Why: fixed numbers and a few lines of copy; today the coach cannot see that Throw numbers lifts their scoring more than his.
3. Who tags' 40% and Skip's dropped calls (G5, G11). Why: copy fixes only; the code already does what the numbers say.
4. The draft stops giving away the true rating beside a scouting range: League Draft price, Full ratings, strengths and traits, Pick for me (list misleading copy 1 to 4). Why: the scouting miss is the one honest risk of a draft pick (listbuild key facts), and a disclosed range beside a leak makes the disclosure false. This is a design change, so it follows the copy-only items.
5. Story cards: the 17 dominant cards get a cost, the 12 misleading Gain/Cost lines are fixed, the 17 silent defaults are shown at the card (S17). Why: many small effects; the silent default is the biggest gap between what he thinks happens and what does.
6. Club gates: season plan, high-performance budget's peak odds, re-sign discount and insult rule, extend-or-wait premium, the board's promise card +8/-10, finals cards' 1 in 5 (club rows 4, 11, 12, 15, 19, 25). Why: real money and results every season, but copy plus a few figures.
7. The contract and club dominant options get a cost (section 5). Why: each needs a design decision on what the cost is, so it comes last.

The order is: size of the measured effect first (1, 2), then copy-only fixes (3), then design changes (4 to 7).

## 7. The gate format to put to the director (he decides)

Every option reads "Upside: ... Downside: ... Odds: ..." in football words. The game never says which is better. Three
ways to write the odds and sizes:

- A (recommended): odds as "about 1 in N", sizes in points, goals, weeks or dollars. Reads like a commentator.
- B: odds as percentages.
- C: both ("about 1 in 5, 20%").
